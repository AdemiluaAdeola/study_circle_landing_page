Chapter 01 — the problem

Two bad options. We're building the third.

Every exam year, Nigerian students get handed the same two choices. Both cost you, in different currencies: one costs understanding, the other costs money and time.

Option A

The past-question app

  • Ten thousand questions, none of them chosen for you
  • Nobody to ask why you got it wrong
  • The same difficulty whether you're struggling or soaring
  • You practise what you already know, because it feels good
Option B

Finding a one-on-one tutor

  • ₦2,000–₦5,000 an hour, paid up front to a stranger
  • Discovery happens in WhatsApp statuses and DMs
  • Scheduling is a part-time job; rescheduling is a full-time one
  • Quality is a coin flip — there is no verification layer
The third option

Both — in one place, with the money held.

Verified tutors you can book from ₦1,000 a session, with the fee escrowed until the session is marked complete. On top of that, an AI practice engine that remembers which topics you keep losing marks on and keeps serving them back — with the syllabus source for every explanation.

Chapter 02 — the loop

Diagnostic → tutor → drill. Then repeat.

Four steps, and each one feeds the next: what you get wrong in the diagnostic changes what the AI serves you, and what the AI notices lands in your tutor's hands before the session starts.

01 Diagnostic

Answer a handful of questions

Each topic gets its own starting difficulty. From the first practice question onwards, nothing you're served is random.

02 Match

Book a verified tutor

Browse by subject, price, rating and availability. Credentials are checked — and the tutor does a short interview — before a profile is ever bookable.

03 Session

Learn in one thread

Chat with your tutor in-app and pull the AI assistant into the same thread when a pattern needs unpacking. Your fee stays in escrow the whole time.

04 The drill

Keep drilling weak spots

Between sessions, the engine keeps returning to the topics you keep missing — with explanations pulled from syllabus content, and sources shown.

What's deliberately not here in v1: no video calls, no group sessions. Chat, explanation and adaptive practice — done properly — before we add anything that needs a reliable 2 Mbps.

Chapter 04 — the money

Every booking is a state machine — and the money follows it.

A booking only ever exists in one of six states. Payment isn't a button, it's a side effect of the transition: held on confirmed, released on completed, refunded when it should be. Click through the states below — this is the same order the walkthrough follows.

    Chapter 05 — the engine

    It explains with the syllabus open, not from memory.

    Ask for practice on a topic and the pipeline retrieves the relevant syllabus passages first, then asks the model to generate a question and explanation grounded in them — returned as validated structured output, and logged so it can be reviewed against a curated exam-question set.

        Adaptive, not random
        
    A difficulty per student, per topic
    
        
    Each topic you touch carries its own difficulty. Get it right and it tightens; get it wrong and the engine steps back until it sticks. A deliberate simple heuristic to start, not pretrained psychometrics.
    
      
    
      
    
        Structured output
        
    Question, options, answer, explanation
    
        
    The model can't hand back free-form prose. It returns a validated object, so a malformed generation never reaches you as a half-question.
    
      
    
      
    
        Auditable
        
    Every generation is logged
    
        
    Prompt, retrieved context and output are stored together — which is how explanation quality can be scored against a set of real JAMB/WAEC questions instead of vibes.

      Chapter 09 — how we'll know
      
      
      Four numbers decide whether this worked.
      
      
      There isn't a single invented statistic on this page — so here is what we'll actually be watching when the pilot runs, and where each number comes from.
      
    
      

    
      
      
      
        
      Booking completion rate
      
        
      Of every booking that gets requested, how many reach completed instead of being abandoned, cancelled or no-showed?
      
        Counted from state transitions — the ledger, not a log line
      
      
      
      
        
      Tutor retention after 3 sessions
      
        
      Do tutors keep teaching past their third session? That's the honest test of whether a marketplace's supply side is actually viable.
      
        Tracked per tutor cohort, never in aggregate
      
      
      
      
        
      Questions answered per student per week
      
        
      Engagement with the practice engine, with no tutor involved. If this stays flat, the AI layer isn't earning the model costs it burns.
      
        Logged server-side per active student
      
      
      
      
        
      Explanation quality score
      
        
      Manually scored against a curated set of real JAMB/WAEC questions — because a plausible explanation and a correct one are different things.
      
        A person reviews it against the stored generation log
      
      
    
      

    
      
      
      Six things we are deliberately not building yet.
      
      
      Scope is a feature. Every one of these is a good idea that would make the pilot worse by existing too early.
      
    
      
    
        
      
      • DeferredLive video tutoring
        Chat, explanation and practice first. Video adds bandwidth, devices and scheduling complexity we haven't earned.
        • 
      
      • DeferredGroup sessions
        One-to-one quality is already hard. It becomes many-sided the moment four students share a room.
        • 
      
      • DeferredEvery subject at once
        Three subjects end to end beats twenty that half work. The next one ships when the last one is finished.
        • 
      
      • DeferredFine-tuned models
        Retrieval plus careful prompting is the v1 approach — cheap to change, and every answer stays traceable to a source.
        • 
      
      • DeferredReferral & affiliate schemes
        Growth machinery bolted onto a product that hasn't proven retention is a distraction with a growth chart attached.
        • 
      
      • DeferredA full admin dashboard
        Just enough tooling to verify tutors by hand and settle disputes. Automation arrives when volume demands it.
        •

      Chapter 10 — questions
      
      
      Asked, answered honestly.
      
      
      Including the ones where the honest answer is "we haven't locked that down yet" — which is the useful kind of answer.
      
    
      

    
      
      
      
        Which subjects does the pilot cover?
        
      Mathematics, Use of English and Physics — through every part of the product: diagnostic, tutor matching, chat, adaptive practice. New subjects are added one at a time, once the one before it works end to end.
      
      
      
      
      
        How are tutors verified?
        
      By hand, for the pilot: credentials are checked and every tutor does a short interview before their profile can be booked. We'd rather over-verify a hundred tutors than under-verify a thousand — but it does mean onboarding is slow on purpose right now.
      
      
      
      
      
        What happens if someone doesn't show up?
        
      The fee sits in escrow from the moment a booking is confirmed until the session is marked complete. If the student doesn't show, the tutor keeps the fee that was already held. If the tutor doesn't show, the student is refunded automatically — no support ticket required. The exact cancellation window is still being finalised and will be published in writing before the first paid session.
      
      
      
      
      
        Is the AI practice really free?
        
      Yes, with a daily question cap. Circle+ (₦1,500/month) removes the cap and adds a weekly weak-spot report. Model calls cost real money per question, so the cap is what keeps the free tier alive — it isn't a trick to upsell you.
      
      
      
      
      
        My network is unreliable. Will this still work?
        
      That's an assumption we build on, not an edge case. Chat queues messages locally and retries when the connection returns, and the practice engine is text rather than video, so a slow link still works. Offline handling lands in the pilot, before the mobile app does.
      
      
      
      
      
        Where does the syllabus content come from?
        
      Still being confirmed. The engine retrieves from a curated syllabus corpus, and we want that sourcing and licensing settled in the open rather than quietly scraped. Until it is, every explanation in the pilot shows which syllabus topic it came from, so a student can check it themselves.
      
      
      
      
      
        Who's building this, and why like this?
        
      A small team in Nigeria, deliberately. StudyCircle doubles as a learning project: every feature is chosen because it forces real depth in backend systems, retrieval-augmented AI, cross-platform frontend work, or optimisation. Depth over breadth — in the product, and in how it's built.
      
      
      
      
      
        Does the demo on this page actually work?
        
      The adaptive selection runs for real — in your browser, with no server involved. The question bank behind it was hand-written for this page, and it says so. Anything simulated on this page is labelled as simulated on the page itself.
